 The discrete logarithm problem becomes trivially easy once two parties are involved
 The primitive root alpha must be kept secret, and any leak instantly reveals the key

The correct answer is:

Intercept Alice's public value and send their own to Bob.

A timing attack against RSA, as described by Kocher, works by:



 A. Directly factoring the public modulus n using a quantum computer

 B. Guessing the plaintext message and checking it against a list of common phrases

 C. Measuring how long decryption takes for different ciphertexts to leak information about the private key, bit by bit

 D. Exploiting a mathematically proven flaw in the RSA encryption formula itself

The correct option is C. Measuring how long decryption takes for different ciphertexts to leak information about the private key, bit by bit.

Why C is correct: Paul Kocher introduced side-channel timing attacks by showing that variations in execution time during modular exponentiation (due to conditional branches or data-dependent operations) reveal the individual bits of the private exponent.

Why the others are incorrect:

A refers to Shor's algorithm, not a timing side-channel attack.

B describes a dictionary attack or brute-force guessing against the message space.

D is incorrect because RSA's underlying mathematical formulation remains sound; the flaw lies entirely in the physical implementation and non-constant-time execution.

NIST's CTR_DRBG, the modern standard block-cipher-based PRNG, operates through which three phases?



 A. Initialize (seed from entropy), Generate (encrypt and increment a counter), and Update (periodically reseed with fresh entropy)

 B. Shuffle, Swap, and Output, exactly as in the RC4 algorithm

 C. Encrypt, Decrypt, and Re-encrypt, exactly as in Triple DES

 D. Select primes, Compute totient, and Generate keys, exactly as in RSA

The correct option is A. Initialize (seed from entropy), Generate (encrypt and increment a counter), and Update (periodically reseed with fresh entropy).

Why A is correct: According to NIST SP 800-90A, CTR_DRBG operates via:

Instantiation / Initialization: Sets the internal state (Key and Value/counter) using seed material derived from an entropy source.

Update / Reseed: Mixes in additional data or fresh entropy to refresh the internal state and provide forward/backward secrecy.

Why the others are incorrect:

B describes the state operations of the RC4 stream cipher (KSA and PRGA).

C describes the 3-key Triple DES operation mode (EDE: Encrypt-Decrypt-Encrypt).

D describes the standard key generation procedure for RSA.
